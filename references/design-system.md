# Dear X, Today Design System

## Purpose

Turn meaningful photos into usable fan-letter stationery: one main image supplies identity and mood, while selected details from every additional image become a coherent, light Instagram-inspired border collage.

## Visual hierarchy

1. Blank writing field
2. Recognizable subject cutout
3. Source-derived decorative cluster
4. Small balancing accents

The blank field is functional content, not leftover space. Judge every decorative decision by whether it protects writing legibility.

## Multi-photo collage system

- Main photo: controls identity, dominant palette, contrast, lighting temperature, and emotional tone.
- Element-source photos: contribute one to three recognizable objects, motifs, textures, or color cues each.
- Selection priority: distinctive personal object → clear foreground object → characteristic pattern or texture → color accent.
- Treatment: isolate or simplify the element, reduce saturation when necessary, then apply the same outline, grain, tape, torn-paper, or soft-shadow language used across the page.
- Placement: group elements into two to four asymmetrical edge clusters with varied scale and slight overlap. Keep the center calm.
- Hierarchy: the main subject remains first; extracted elements provide memory cues rather than competing scenes.

Do not preserve the original color cast of every source photo independently. Regrade all extracted elements toward the main photo so the finished page feels designed in one sitting.

## Page zones

- Safe writing field: central 65%–88%, depending on the user's blank-area request.
- Identity zone: one lower corner or side, normally 15%–30% of page area.
- Decoration zones: outer 8%–18% on the top, sides, and lower corners.
- Quiet boundary: leave visible breathing room between decoration clusters and the writing field.

## Source-to-decoration translation

- Clothing colors → ribbons, petals, stars, small color blocks, or border accents.
- Setting → simplified flowers, clouds, lights, leaves, architectural lines, tickets, or weather motifs.
- Pose or gesture → directional curves, sparkles, trailing lines, or a nearby object cluster.
- Lighting → warm or cool paper tint and accent contrast.
- Repeating garment pattern → sparse repeated micro-motifs; never blanket the page.
- Personal objects → one or two recognizable edge accents, not central illustrations.

## Style profiles

### Soft scrapbook

Clean subject cutout, tape-like shapes without realistic shadows, stickers, torn-edge motifs, doodles, soft source-derived colors.

### Light Instagram collage — default for multiple photos

Airy asymmetrical layout, restrained torn-paper pieces, translucent tape, small instant-photo edges, postage or ticket fragments, thin hand-drawn accents, gentle grain, and compact sticker-like cutouts. Use one palette and one edge treatment throughout. Avoid thick object piles, dark heavy frames, or dense physical flat-lay styling.

### Clean editorial

Large whitespace, controlled photo cutout, geometric accents, fine lines, sparse symbols, restrained palette.

### Dreamy

Pale tint, soft flowers, stars, clouds, ribbons, gentle glow limited to edge decorations; keep the writing field flat and clean.

### Retro print

Muted colors, stamps, ticket shapes, simple halftone accents, old-paper tint; avoid dirty stains or texture inside the writing field.

### Seasonal

Use only the requested or clearly visible season. Keep seasonal motifs subordinate to the subject and writing area.

## Negative constraints

Do not generate a poster, magazine cover, photo album page, phone wallpaper, greeting card with a prewritten message, or a realistic photographed sheet of paper. Do not fill the center merely to improve visual balance. Do not paste every source image as a separate photo tile, preserve clashing color casts, repeat faces, or let supporting elements overpower the main subject.
